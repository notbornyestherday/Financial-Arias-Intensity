| D-022 | 2026-10-28 | Rule for the full-pipeline H1b null check (extends D-011), fixed before it is run (`scripts/sim_h1b_null_pipeline.py`). Efficient one-minute returns have t(4) innovations, a U-shaped intraday profile, a random daily volatility level (lognormal, SD 0.5 in logs) and 0-3 bursts a day of random start, length 3-30 minutes and volatility multiplier exp(U(0, 2.5)), as in D-011. Observed prices add Roll bid-ask bounce: each minute's close prints at the bid or the ask at random. Four scenarios, 500 samples each. A: half-spread constant at 1 bp (about five times SPY's typical, on purpose). B1: half-spread proportional to local volatility (1 bp at the typical level). B2: half-spread x10 inside bursts and 1 bp otherwise; burst volatility is x1 to x12 (x4.5 on average), so in most bursts spreads widen more than volatility. C: as A, but deseasonalized with the pipeline's own trailing estimate (D-020 settings, 60-day warm-up) instead of the true profile. A scenario passes if, for both W50 and D5-75, (i) the test rejects in at most 7.5% of samples (5% plus about 2.5 Monte Carlo SEs) and (ii) the mean slope is within a quarter of the slope's across-sample SD of zero. B1 is a control: it should pass, and a failure there points to a bug. If any scenario fails, no real H1b result is looked at until a fix is agreed and logged (candidates: 10-minute base blocks; a spread control such as Corwin-Schultz). | D-011 used independent returns with no microstructure. Bid-ask bounce lowers VR: each 5-minute block carries its own bounce, while a 30-minute block keeps only the bounce at its two ends. If spreads are constant, or widen in proportion to volatility, every day's VR shifts by the same amount whatever the timing of its energy, so the slope on ln W50 is unaffected. If spreads widen more than volatility during bursts, only concentrated days get the extra drop, which mimics H1b; that is the realistic threat, because spreads blow out in liquidity events. D-011 also only checked that slopes were within about 2 SE of zero; the rejection rate tests whether the Newey-West SEs are right when concentrated days, with fewer effective blocks, have noisier VR. Exploratory comparison (`scripts/explore_h1b_fixes.py`, seed 2301, 300 samples, not registered): VR(30/10) halves the B2 problem but still rejects in 38% of samples; VR* rejects in 5.3% and keeps about two-thirds of the registered test's power when prices really do reverse (39% vs 60%, transient component with a 10-minute half-life). Scenario D tests the one assumption the fix relies on: if prints keep to one side for minutes at a time, bounce is correlated across more than one block boundary. Decided before any H1 result. |
